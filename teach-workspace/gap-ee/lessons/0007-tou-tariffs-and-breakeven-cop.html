0007 · TOU Tariffs & the Breakeven-COP Table

gap-ee week 2, day 7 · 45-60 min · pencil, calculator, vn_context.json · mission

This is the lesson the whole course exists for: reproducing — by hand, from raw VND rates and the FX rate — the engagement's core finding that there is no single breakeven COP. No precomputed USD numbers allowed: you start from vn_context.json's 3,398 / 1,833 / 1,190 VND per kWh and convert everything yourself.

Mission link. The table you build here (1.88 / 2.90 / 5.37) is the yardstick the capstone is judged against: REopt's heat-pump-plus-storage answer must be consistent with it. If a design conclusion ever contradicts this arithmetic, distrust the model first.

1. The formula

A heat pump "breaks even" against the coal boiler when its delivered-heat cost equals coal's delivered-heat cost. Heat delivered per kWh of electricity is COP × 293.071/1000 MMBtu (since 1 MMBtu = 293.071 kWh). Setting the two costs equal and solving for COP:

COP_breakeven = (P_elec × 293.071) / (P_coal / η_boiler)

  P_elec      grid electricity price, USD/kWh, for the tariff period being evaluated
  293.071     kWh per MMBtu (unit-conversion constant)
  P_coal      delivered coal price, USD/MMBtu as-fired ($5.30)
  η_boiler    boiler efficiency as a fraction (0.75)
  P_coal/η    coal DELIVERED-heat cost = $7.067/MMBtu useful heat

Intuition check before any arithmetic: the heat pump converts electricity to heat at rate COP; it beats $7.067/MMBtu coal exactly when its electricity is cheap enough that (COP × kWh bought) costs less than one MMBtu-equivalent of coal heat. Higher tariff ⇒ higher COP needed. That is the whole finding, waiting for numbers.

2. Step 1 — VND → USD yourself

FX = 26,250 VND/USD   (vn_context.json → fx.vnd_per_usd)

peak     : 3,398 ÷ 26,250 = $0.12945/kWh
normal   : 1,833 ÷ 26,250 = $0.06983/kWh
off_peak : 1,190 ÷ 26,250 = $0.04533/kWh

These are excl.-VAT rates — correct for a manufacturer that recovers input VAT. Do not add the 8% unless building a cash-flow view.

3. Step 2 — the three breakeven COPs

denominator:  P_coal / η = 5.30 / 0.75 = $7.0667/MMBtu

off-peak : (0.04533 × 293.071) / 7.0667 = 13.2844 / 7.0667 = 1.879  ≈ 1.88
normal   : (0.06983 × 293.071) / 7.0667 = 20.4645 / 7.0667 = 2.895  ≈ 2.90
peak     : (0.12945 × 293.071) / 7.0667 = 37.9380 / 7.0667 = 5.369  ≈ 5.37

4. Step 3 — what the table says about a real machine

The dye-house heat pump from lesson 0002 runs at actual COP 3.11:

  • Off-peak: 3.11 > 1.88 → wins big. Heat at (0.04533×293.071)/3.11 ≈ $4.27/MMBtu vs coal's $7.07 — about −40%.
  • Normal: 3.11 > 2.90 → wins modestly: (0.06983×293.071)/3.11 ≈ $6.58/MMBtu, −7%.
  • Peak: 3.11 ≪ 5.37 → loses badly: (0.12945×293.071)/3.11 ≈ $12.20/MMBtu, +73%.

A single blended annual rate (~$0.072/kWh) gives one breakeven near 3.3 — which reads as "heat pumps are marginal." That conclusion is wrong because the load is not flat across the day: run the machine off-peak and through normal hours, store heat, and ride through the 5-hour peak. The design answer is heat pump + thermal storage — the exact hypothesis the capstone (0011) hands to REopt.

Sensitive input: the FX rate (26,250 VND/USD) moves every USD-denominated result one-for-one with a 5% move; the coal price rests on a 14-Aug-2026 Newcastle benchmark. If you are reading this much later than 2026-08-25, re-check vn_context.json before reusing these numbers in live work — but keep the practice copy frozen so it keeps matching the baseline it extends.

Practice quiz

  1. In the breakeven formula, why divide P_coal by η_boiler?

    • To convert fuel volume into mass
    • To discount future fuel prices to present value
    • Because only η of each unit of fuel becomes useful heat — dividing converts fuel cost into delivered-heat cost per MMBtu
    • To account for transmission losses on the grid side

    $5.30 buys one MMBtu as-fired, but only 0.75 MMBtu reaches the process. Delivered-heat cost = 5.30/0.75 = $7.07/MMBtu — the correct bar for the heat pump to clear.

  2. Converting from raw VND: what does 1,190 VND/kWh become at 26,250 VND/USD?

    • $0.05210/kWh
    • $0.04533/kWh
    • $0.04831/kWh
    • $0.03960/kWh

    1,190 ÷ 26,250 = 0.045333… → $0.04533/kWh, the off-peak rate. Doing the division yourself from raw VND (not reading precomputed USD values) is the point of the exercise — transcription-free provenance.

  3. During peak hours, what must a heat pump's COP exceed to undercut coal?

    • About 2.9
    • About 1.9
    • About 5.37
    • Exactly 3.11 always

    (0.12945 × 293.071)/7.0667 ≈ 5.37. Practical machines sit at COP 3–4, so peak-running is economically hopeless without storage — hence heat pump + TES.

  4. The COP-3.11 dye-house heat pump delivers heat off-peak at roughly…

    • $4.27/MMBtu — about 40% below coal's $7.07
    • $6.58/MMBtu — slightly above coal
    • $12.20/MMBtu — far above coal
    • $2.24/MMBtu — cheaper than anything else

    (0.04533 × 293.071)/3.11 ≈ 4.27. Off-peak is where the machine shines; normal hours give $6.58 (−7%), and $12.20 (+73%) is the peak figure. $2.24 is the discredited heat_pump_model LCOH — never quote it (lesson 0008).

  5. Why would quoting ONE blended breakeven (~3.3) mislead the design?

    • It overstates off-peak profitability
    • Blended rates are mathematically invalid under any TOU tariff
    • It hides the hour-to-hour spread: COP 3.11 looks marginal overall while actually winning 7,200 h/yr and losing only the 1,560 peak hours
    • Because boilers are priced monthly, not annually

    The blended view says "marginal"; the hourly view says "win off-peak and normal, avoid peak — add thermal storage." Different conclusions, different equipment. This is why the scenario models the tariff as an 8760 vector.

  6. Coal price rises to $6.50/MMBtu (η still 0.75). What happens to the three breakeven COPs?

    • They rise proportionally (e.g. peak → ~6.59)
    • They fall by exactly 0.75 each
    • They fall (e.g. peak → 5.30×... specifically (0.12945×293.071)/(6.50/0.75) ≈ 4.38): dearer coal lowers the COP bar
    • Unchanged — coal price doesn't enter the formula

    Breakeven COP ∝ P_elec/(P_coal/η): dearer coal shrinks the denominator, lowering the bar. This sensitivity is why vn_context.json flags the coal price (wide trader range $3.8–7.0/MMBtu) as the number to nail down per site.

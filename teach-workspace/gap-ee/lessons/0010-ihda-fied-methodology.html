0010 · IHDA/FIED Methodology

gap-ee week 2, days 9-10 · 30-45 min reading · read-only lesson · mission

The last two tools are not software you run but references you think with: NLR's Industrial Heat Demand Analysis (IHDA) repo and the Foundational Industrial Energy Data (FIED) characterization. Their datasets are American to the core — and permanently unusable as numbers for Vietnam. Their method, however, answers a question you will face on every new supplier site: when nobody knows how much heat goes where, how do you estimate the split by temperature level?

Mission link. The engagement's rule (tools/README.md §5): "take the method, never the numbers." This lesson is that rule, unpacked — plus the honest statement that no Vietnamese or Indonesian equivalent dataset exists yet. Building one, supplier by supplier, is exactly what the data-collection workbook is for.

1. The problem the method solves

A garment factory says: "we use a 10 bar(g) steam header for everything." But dye baths need 95°C hot water, dryers want 120–160°C air, ironing stations want low-pressure steam. If all heat is booked at header temperature, every electrification calculation is wrong: a heat pump that could serve the 60–95°C band gets judged against 184°C steam economics and fails on paper while it would have won in reality. You need the load split by temperature band: how many MMBtu/yr below 100°C, between 100–200°C, above.

2. How Enduse_Calc.py does it (read the file)

Open C:\Users\tukum\Downloads\ee-heat\tools\reference\Industrial-Heat-Demand-Analysis\Enduse_Calc.py. The approach, stripped of its US plumbing:

  1. End-use taxonomy first. A fixed list of industrial end uses (process heating subtypes, HVAC, machine drive...) — the vocabulary every estimate must speak.
  2. Prior per end use × industry. For each (end use, industry sector) pair, assign a typical temperature range — e.g. a priors table saying "textile dyeing sits at X–Y°C". These priors come from US GHGRP/MECS data (last updated 2018) — the part that does NOT transfer.
  3. Allocate total fuel to bands. Multiply sector-level energy consumption by the end-use shares and their temperature ranges → fuel demand resolved into temperature bins, which is precisely what clean-heat technology screening needs: each technology has a maximum temperature lift, so each bin has a candidate set.
  4. Sum to sanity. Bins roll up to the site's known total — conservation as a checksum.

FIED (foundational-industry-energy-data/) contributes the second half of the pattern: unit-level characterization — describing each thermal unit (boiler, oven, heater) with capacity, fuel, utilization, age — the structure to copy verbatim into any supplier survey.

3. Why the numbers can NEVER be quoted for Vietnam

  • Different data universes: GHGRP covers large US emitters' reported emissions; MECS is a US Census/EIA survey of US plants. Coverage bias, not measurement.
  • Different industry mix: US textile processing ≠ Vietnamese CMT garment sector; wet-processing intensity differs enormously.
  • Different vintages: 2018 or older; Vietnam's industrial base is growing and retooling faster than any US dataset rotates.
  • No VN/ID equivalent exists — verified during localization (the only tool in the stack where localization was impossible). Quoting GHGRP-derived percentages for a Binh Duong supplier would be fabrication with citations.

The defensible workflow: use the taxonomy + binning structure, seed priors from supplier data as the workbook collects it (nameplate ratings, steam-meter readings, dye-house schedules), and let site reality replace priors over time. Where a prior must stand in before data lands, label it as placeholder explicitly — same discipline as vn_context.json's UNVERIFIED PLACEHOLDER blocks for Indonesia.

Practice quiz

  1. What question does the IHDA methodology exist to answer?

    • Which boiler vendor offers the best price
    • How to optimize a TOU tariff vector hour by hour
    • How to split an industrial site's heat demand by temperature range/end use when direct measurements don't exist
    • How to forecast next year's coal price

    Temperature-band resolution of heat demand is the product. It determines which clean-heat technologies are even candidates per band — the input every later sizing step needs.

  2. Why does booking ALL heat at 10 bar(g) header conditions break heat-pump screening?

    • Because heat pumps cannot connect to steam headers physically
    • Low-temperature loads (60–95°C dye/wash) get judged against high-grade steam economics, hiding wins a heat pump would actually deliver
    • Steam headers make metering impossible
    • It doesn't — header temperature IS the correct basis

    A COP-3.11 machine serving 95°C water competes well (lesson 0007); against 184°C steam economics it never can. Mis-binned load = systematically wrong screening.

  3. In Enduse_Calc.py, which ingredient actually transfers to Vietnam?

    • The structure: end-use taxonomy × industry priors → temperature-bin allocation, with totals conserved as a checksum
    • The GHGRP-derived emission factors
    • The 2018 end-use share tables themselves
    • The MECS survey questionnaire mailing lists

    Method yes, numbers no. The priors' provenance (US GHGRP/MECS ≤2018) makes them invalid for VN/ID sites even though the machinery consuming them is perfectly reusable.

  4. What does FIED add to the pattern?

    • Unit-level characterization structure — describe each thermal unit (capacity, fuel, utilization, age)
    • A Vietnamese industrial dataset collected in 2024
    • A tariff optimization engine
    • Weather files for Southeast Asia

    FIED = how to describe a facility's thermal units consistently. Copy that survey structure; ignore the US values filling it.

  5. You need a dye-house temperature prior TODAY but the supplier hasn't answered. Defensible move?

    • Quote the US textile share from Enduse_Calc.py silently
    • Skip the site until full metering exists
    • Use a stated placeholder assumption, explicitly labelled, flagged for replacement by supplier data — mirroring vn_context.json's UNVERIFIED PLACEHOLDER discipline
    • Assume 100% of heat goes to dyeing to be safe

    Labelled placeholders keep analyses moving without laundering assumptions into facts. Silent US-number reuse is the exact failure mode tools/README.md §5 forbids.

  6. Why is localization impossible for IHDA/FIED (unlike every other tool)?

    • Their licenses forbid modification
    • They require internet access to US servers
    • They ARE US datasets by construction (EPA GHGRP, EIA MECS) — no Vietnamese or Indonesian equivalent exists to substitute
    • Their code only runs on Linux

    MEASUR/REopt/E+/heat_pump_model had US defaults you could override with real values. Here the default IS the dataset; nothing domestic replaces it yet — hence method-only import, and the long-game value of building VN priors from actual suppliers.

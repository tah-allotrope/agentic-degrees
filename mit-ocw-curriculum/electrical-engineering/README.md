# Electrical Engineering Track — MIT OCW

Free EE degree-equivalent: 9 courses spanning circuits → signals → electromagnetics →
microelectronics → power. Good for both classic EE depth and the power/electronics career path.

## Order & rationale

1. **6.01SC Intro to EECS I** — integrated circuits+signals+systems with Python and labs. (Spring 2011)
2. **6.002 Circuits & Electronics** — KVL/KCL, op-amps, diodes, transistors, digital logic. (Spring 2007)
3. **6.003 Signals & Systems** — LTI systems, Fourier/Laplace/z-transforms; the math of EE. (Fall 2011)
4. **6.007 Electromagnetic Energy: Motors to Lasers** — fields, Maxwell, motors, fiber optics. (Spring 2011)
5. **6.012 Microelectronic Devices & Circuits** — semiconductor physics, MOSFETs, IC design. (Fall 2009)
6. **6.061 Intro to Electric Power Systems** — transformers, per-unit, power flow, grids. (Spring 2011)
7. **6.685 Electric Machines** — motors & generators, drives, applications. (Fall 2013)
8. **6.622 Power Electronics** — converters, PWM, control; core of modern power engineering. (Spring 2023)
9. **6.071J Electronics, Signals & Measurement** — hands-on lab instrumentation; do early if you like hardware. (Spring 2006)

## Milestones

- After 1-3: solid fundamentals — can read schematics, analyze circuits and signals.
- After 4-5: EM + devices — the "real EE" core.
- After 6-8: power engineering specialization — grid, machines, converters (very relevant for
  Vietnam energy sector work: DPPA, BESS, renewables integration).
- 6.071J anytime after 1 for practical lab skills.

## Tips

- 6.002 and 6.003 are the two courses that make or break the track — spend extra time on them.
- Use the exam archives (with solutions) as the real checkpoint.
- The power sequence (6.061 → 6.685 → 6.622) maps directly onto utility-scale solar/BESS/DPPA
  technical diligence — the professional payoff is immediate.
- Videos: stream from each course's `videos.txt`, or `yt-dlp -a videos.txt`.
